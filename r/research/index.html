Coherence Engine™
Instability, Drift, and Recovery Research

A public-facing summary of six synthetic experiments and one real-world energy study examining whether composite coherence indicators can surface instability, hidden drift, and recovery behavior.

Proprietary Coherence Scoring Architecture

The Coherence Engine™ combines multiple system-state indicators into a composite measure designed to identify drift, instability, and recovery dynamics. The underlying calculation, weighting, gating, and decision logic are proprietary and patent pending.

Public materials describe the research purpose, datasets, evaluation approach, and reported outputs without publishing the core equation or implementation logic.

Synthetic experiments
Experiment 01

Coherence response across increasing noise

Tests whether the composite score changes at a repeatable transition as noise increases.

Reported observation: a sharp response occurred near σ ≈ 0.061 in this synthetic configuration.

Experiment 02

Recovery dynamics after perturbation

Examines whether the architecture differentiates recovery behavior after a controlled shock.

Reported observation: recovery-rate changes produced distinguishable composite-score trajectories across the tested conditions.

Experiment 03

Hidden drift before visible failure

Compares early drift detection against a rolling-variance baseline in a synthetic degradation sequence.

Experiment 04

Shock response under different coherence conditions

Explores relationships among initial condition, peak deviation, and return behavior following a shock.

Reported observation: lower-coherence conditions generally showed larger disturbance effects or slower return behavior, though the relationship was not perfectly monotonic.

Experiment 05

Cross-system generalization check

Tests behavior across sinusoidal, chaotic, piecewise, and stochastic signal families.

Reported observation: output magnitudes differed substantially across signal families. The reported coefficient of variation was 1.010, indicating that calibration or normalization remains necessary before claiming cross-system invariance.
